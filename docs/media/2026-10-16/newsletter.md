# Planning Side or Practice Side: Placing an AI Tool Request

**Subject:** Planning side or practice side for an AI tool?

**Preview:** A proposed call for school leaders: keep an AI tool on the planning side, or allow student practice help with an attempt-first norm.

## The Call
This week's proposed call: when an AI tool request arrives, decide which side of the classroom it sits on. In a Google for Education post, a teacher describes using an AI tool as a planning partner. Separately, Khan Academy reports that its tutor tool holds back the answer before a student submits one, offering questions and hints. That post does not label the tool as AI, so treat it as a help pattern, not an AI finding. Pick a default side, name the learning problem, and write down what help students may ask for and when.

## Two Choices

### Planning side: AI tools stay with teachers for now
A possible benefit is that teachers decide what reaches students while staff build shared habits. A possible cost is that students get no shared school norm for help tools yet, and staff may learn little about how student-facing help behaves. These are proposed considerations, not observed results.

### Practice side: student help allowed after a real attempt
A possible benefit is a clear norm meant to protect student thinking, and teachers may see more of where students get stuck. A possible cost is setup time. Teachers would need a way to see the attempt, and a tool may not follow the norm on its own. These are proposed considerations, not observed results.

## What Changed

- Khan Academy reports that before a student submits an answer, its tutor tool holds back the answer, asks questions and offers hints. This is the publisher's description of its own product, not an independently measured effect. [Source](https://blog.khanacademy.org/student-motivation-hard-problem/)

## The Five Minute Move
Open your next tool request, or pick a tool your staff uses. Write one sentence naming the learning problem it is meant to address. Then ask a student-style question in the tool and note what it does first: a hint, a question, or a full answer. Leave names and student details out of the prompt. Compare what you saw with the side you want. If they do not match, consider adding it to a staff meeting agenda. This is a proposed check, not a tested method.

## Steal This
Draft help norm for our classrooms, proposed for staff discussion and not yet adopted. In this class, AI help comes after your own attempt. First, try the problem and show your work. Second, if you are stuck, ask for a hint or a question, not the answer. Third, once you have turned in your attempt, you may ask what went wrong, then try a similar problem on your own. Teachers may use AI tools to plan lessons and will tell you when they do. This norm is a starting point. We will look at how it is working and adjust it together.

## A Question to Consider
A question to consider: if a student asked a help tool about tonight's practice, would the tool's first move match what your teachers would want?

## The One Thing
Pick one tool your staff uses, ask it one practice question the way a student would, and write down whether its first response is a hint, a question, or an answer.
