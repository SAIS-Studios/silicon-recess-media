The Call

This week's call is about one checkpoint. When an AI tool suggests what a student practices next, decide whether a teacher sees that suggestion before the student does. Khan Academy reports that its planned interim assessments are designed this way, with teachers reviewing recommended paths before they appear in student accounts. That is a publisher description, not a measured effect. The same publisher also argues students need more individual practice. So the real decision is how to keep a person in the loop without slowing practice to a crawl.

Two Choices

Review every suggestion before release

Teachers keep full control over what students see next, but review time adds up across classes, and practice may stall whenever a teacher falls behind.

Spot check with a set review window

Practice keeps moving and teachers can focus on patterns across a class, but some poorly matched suggestions may reach students before anyone notices them.

What Changed

- Khan Academy reports that its planned interim assessments are designed so teachers review recommended learning paths before those paths reach student accounts. Source

- Khan Academy describes a planned feature that gives students a chance to explain their reasoning during an assessment, with the publisher saying educators receive insights from those conversations. Source

- In a separate Khan Academy post, the publisher's chief learning officer argues that a very common school problem is students not getting enough individual practice. Source

The Five Minute Move

Pick one AI tool in your building that suggests student practice. Keep it to one tool so the task stays small. Open it as a teacher would and trace where a suggestion goes. Note whether it lands in student accounts directly or waits for a teacher. Write one sentence describing what you found. Then write a second sentence proposing who could review suggestions and how quickly. Share both sentences with a colleague and ask what you missed. Treat the result as a draft to discuss, not a finished procedure.

Steal This

Draft for discussion: When a digital tool suggests what a student should practice next, we propose that a teacher reviews the suggestion before it reaches the student. The goal is fit, not delay. To keep practice moving, we propose a review window that our team agrees on together. Teachers may approve, adjust, or replace a suggestion. If a written insight about student reasoning comes with a suggestion, we propose checking it against the student's actual work before acting on it. We will revisit this draft after trying it and change what does not work for our students and staff.
